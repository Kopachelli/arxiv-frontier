## Who to ask

arXiv endorsement must come from someone with posting rights in the target category
(`cs.DL` primary). Best candidates are corresponding authors whose work our results directly
extend — they can judge the paper on sight, and our findings are useful to them:

1. **Izumi Takahara / Teruyasu Mizoguchi** — *Toward Auditable AI Scientists* (arXiv:2607.09195).
   Closest intellectual neighbours; we supply the field-scale measurement their argument
   implies but does not have.
2. **Jingjie Ning et al.** — *Auto Research for Materials: Auditable AI-Scientist Workflows
   with Held-Out Transfer* (arXiv:2607.17100). We identify held-out transfer as present in
   only 5% of the field; their paper is one of the exemplars.
3. **Hongmin Li** — *The Calibration Turn in AI-Assisted Research* (arXiv:2606.31273).
   Our Discussion extends the evidence-licensing framework directly.

Check each person's arXiv listings for cs.DL activity before asking; endorsement is
category-specific, and a cs.AI-only author cannot endorse for cs.DL. If none are cs.DL-active,
the fallback is a scientometrics/meta-research author, or submitting to `cs.CY` instead.

Only one endorser is needed. Ask one at a time, not all three.

## Notes for sending

- Attach the PDF; do not make the endorser hunt for it.
- Tailor the "I am contacting you specifically because" sentence to the actual paper. A
  generic request reads as a mass mailing and will be ignored.
- arXiv's endorsement mechanism: the endorser receives a code from arXiv and enters it; ask
  them to endorse rather than sending them a code yourself.
- If declined or unanswered after ~2 weeks, move to the next name. The Zenodo DOI means the
  work is already public and citable regardless of the outcome.
