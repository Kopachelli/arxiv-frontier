\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Autonomous AI research systems --- ``AI scientists'' --- went from a handful of preprints to
a substantial literature in under three years. We map that literature systematically: from
\nHarvest{} arXiv records we screen \nScreened{} candidates and code \nCorpus{} papers
(January 2024--July 2026) on ten dimensions covering system type, demonstrated autonomy,
research-lifecycle coverage, domain, evaluation method, auditability mechanisms, artifact
release, claim strength, and the role assigned to humans.

Two things distinguish this map. First, the object: we ask not what these systems can do but
\emph{what evidence they offer that they did it}. Quarterly output grew roughly
\growthFactor{}-fold (\firstQn{} papers in \firstQ{} to \peakQn{} in \peakQ{}), yet
\pctAuditNone{} of the corpus provides no auditability mechanism of any kind, only
\pctHeldOut{} test a selected result outside the loop that produced it, and
\pctHumanUnspec{} never state what humans did. The imbalance is sharpest exactly where it
matters most: among papers claiming the AI produced new scientific knowledge,
\pctDiscNoAudit{} provide no auditability mechanism.

Second, the instrument: the review was conducted by a frontier language model, and its
process was logged rather than smoothed. That log is data. Two frontier models screening
the same corpus under the same protocol disagreed on \pctAdjudicate{} of decisions
($\kappa = \kappaThree{}$), and the disagreements were not noise --- they clustered on seven
boundary classes where the field itself has not settled what counts as an AI scientist,
forcing a documented protocol amendment mid-review. We also record measured failure rates
for silent item-dropping in structured extraction, a workflow that reported success having
done nothing, and a pipeline defect that fed empty inputs to a reasoning step. We argue that
the auditability deficit we measure in the literature and the failure modes we logged in
ourselves are the same problem, and that they are tractable: every one of our own failures
was caught by a cheap reconciliation check, and none would have been visible in the
finished paper.
\end{abstract}
