# Phase V protocol — blind cross-model verification

Written 2026-07-26, **before any verification was run**. Specification by Khristian
Kopachelli (`notes/roadmap.md`); design and preregistered analysis by Claude Fable 5.

---

## 1. Question

Paper 1's corpus was produced by two frontier models (Claude Fable 5, Claude Opus 5). Two
things about that instrument are unknown and measurable:

- **RQ-V1 (reliability).** How much do independent frontier models agree when they re-do the
  same screening and coding work from the same instructions?
- **RQ-V2 (error detection).** How often does a model, auditing recorded decisions, correctly
  identify ones that are wrong?
- **RQ-V3 (self-preference).** Blinded to provenance, does a model evaluate its own prior
  output more favourably than another model's? To our knowledge this has not been measured in
  a systematic-review setting.

RQ-V3 is the reason the design is a 2×2 rather than a simple re-run.

## 2. Design

Two arms, reported separately and never averaged (they answer different questions).

### Arm 1 — independent re-doing (reliability + self-preference)

A fixed random sample of Paper 1's screened corpus is re-screened and re-coded from scratch by
each model, with no access to prior decisions.

|  | re-done by Fable 5 | re-done by Opus 5 |
|---|---|---|
| originally decided by Fable 5 (Pass A) | self-family | cross-model |
| originally decided by Opus 5 (Pass B) | cross-model | self-family |

Agreement with the original decision is compared across the diagonal (same model) and the
off-diagonal (different model). A self-preference effect appears as higher agreement on the
diagonal **when the verifier does not know whose work it is**.

### Arm 2 — audit of recorded decisions (error detection)

Each model receives paper records with a decision attached and is asked to identify decisions
it judges incorrect. Critically, the sample contains **deliberately corrupted decisions**
(see §4), so the arm yields a true detection rate rather than only a disagreement rate.

## 3. Blinding — enforced mechanically, not by instruction

1. Verifier prompts contain no model names, no reference to Paper 1, no authorship
   information, and no statement that the decisions under review were AI-produced.
2. Verifiers receive no access to this repository, the process logs, or this protocol.
3. Provenance labels are stripped from all inputs at export time by
   `code/phase_v.py`; the mapping from item to producing model is held only in a key file
   that is not shown to any verifier.
4. Prompts are byte-identical across conditions except for the paper records themselves.

**Style leakage is expected and is itself measured.** Justification text may carry a model's
stylistic fingerprint. Rather than attempt to normalise it away, Arm 1 includes an explicit
probe: verifiers are asked, for a subset, to guess whether they authored the original
decision. Above-chance identification is a reportable result and a caveat on RQ-V3.

## 4. Corrupted decisions (Arm 2 ground truth)

10% of Arm-2 items carry a deliberately altered decision, generated mechanically:

- `FLIP`: include ↔ exclude, justification left untouched (creates an internal contradiction).
- `REASON_SWAP`: decision retained, reason code replaced with an incompatible one.
- `CODE_DRIFT`: a coded dimension replaced with a value the abstract does not support.

Corruptions are generated by script with a fixed seed and recorded in a key file before any
verifier sees the data. A verifier that flags a corrupted item scores a true positive; one
that flags an uncorrupted item is scored as a disagreement, adjudicated afterwards, and only
counted as a false positive if adjudication upholds the original.

**Corruption is confined to Phase V inputs.** The published corpus is never modified; Phase V
operates on copies.

## 5. Preregistered analysis

- Arm 1: percentage agreement and Cohen's κ, per condition; self-preference as the
  diagonal-minus-off-diagonal difference in agreement, with a permutation test over items.
- Arm 2: sensitivity (corrupted items flagged / corrupted items) and false-positive rate
  (upheld flags on uncorrupted items / uncorrupted items), per model.
- Authorship-identification probe: accuracy against a 50% chance baseline.
- All per-item results released as CSV, as in Paper 1.

## 6. Stopping rule and honesty commitments

The sample size, corruption rate, seed, and analyses above are fixed here. If a result is
unflattering to either model — including the model conducting this study — it is reported
unchanged. If Arm 2 finds errors in Paper 1's published corpus, they are reported publicly
and the Zenodo record is updated with a corrected version; the original version remains
retrievable, as Zenodo versioning requires.

We note the obvious limitation in advance: both verifiers are Claude-family models, so this
measures *cross-model* rather than *independent* agreement, and a shared systematic error is
invisible to the entire design. That is a bound on what Phase V can establish, and it is
stated in advance rather than discovered afterwards.
